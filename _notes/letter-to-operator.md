I have been scribbling a lot of anecdotes. Some of them have been useful for making sense of myself and the world. But like any artist, I am prone to becoming a monomaniac, so lost in the details that I forget about the whole picture.

So after a long time deliberating what to write and how, I sat down to finish the painting. 

I kept coming back to the vocation of this essay in the shape of a letter. 
So this is a letter, written by the passenger to the operator.

---

## 1. The big system

I believe in the butterfly effect, and that too, in the simplest form - everything influences everything. 

If you could trace every cause to every effect, you could predict the future precisely.
You can't. Because you don't know what you don't know. There are variables in your life you have accounted for, and some not, and many of them will stay hidden for your entire lifetime.

There are two things I derive from this, 

First is that control is mostly an illusion. Much of life is flukes. The right posture towards life is to make the best of whatever arrives.

And second is - you matter more than you think. What you project into the world influences it more than you believe. You can't control the system, but you are one of its inputs.

---

## 2. The aspects you can tend to

I built this pentagonal lens from the aspects of life that I frequented. I expect to reread this in some years, maybe scoff a little too, and rewrite it and update the ideas here.

### 1. Life is short

Time is the one resource distributed equitably; everyone gets the same rate. But everyone experiences those hours differently, by chosing where they spend their attention.

Every system has a resource it cannot run without. Food, water and air for the body. Capital for an economy. For the human mind, it is attention.

Learning to allocate attention is a muscle, and it is the muscle that the remaining letter depends on. 

### 2. Relationships are a garden

To be human is to be known. Your identity - the narrative of self - gets its meaning from the people who see it. You cannot construct a self alone.

To live is to live with people. If someone tells you it's possible to do without them, don't trust them. They're ill, heartbroken, or sent by Skynet.

Treat the people in your life like a garden. Some plants need more of you than others. Some will stay for decades; some will leave before you've learnt their names. Some nourish you, some are simply beautiful, and nearly all of them will surprise you.

Because attention is finite, you cannot water everything. You will have to choose, over and over, between building new beds and tending old ones. 

Don't let that choice be made by fear. And don't let age quietly convince you that the garden is finished - a healthy garden is one that is still surprising you.

To tend a garden: listen. Listen to your plants well, and be kind.

### 3. Gardens don't grow the way you planned

Your mind is a prediction machine. It loves predictability because predictability signals safety. But every model you carry, of a person, a job, a plan, a version of yourself - comes with a possibility of not happening.

When it doesn't happen, you will grieve. And, that is okay! You were expecting a future, and a different one showed up. Grieve it. Be kind to yourself while you do.

Then adapt.

Growth almost always lives behind friction. Every instinct will tell you to avoid discomfort. But learn to accept it.

And one hard-won caveat: when it comes to other people, you cannot initiate their change. You can only inspire it, by being what you are. Afterall, their garden is theirs.

### 4. The machine that does all of this needs fuel

Everything above — attention, tending, adapting — is executed by a body and a mind. This is the most underrated part of the whole system, and we aren’t educated well how to care for it.

**The body** is how you sense the world and move through it. To keep it running for a long time, chase the golden snitch - muscle, mobility, endurance. 

**The mind** is the control room — the GPS of sorts. Two things I've learnt about it - 

You have full agency over your *feelings*. Nobody gets to pick them for you.

But *emotions* are different. Emotions are signals — information your intellect hasn't managed to put into words yet. (Yes, emotions ≠ feelings. It took me a while to get it.) Trust them. Treat them as data with a lot of sanctity. Once you start listening, they tell you which direction to move and how hard.

**Both need fuel, and you will forget to refuel.**

The body's fuel is food. Eat for the body, and sometimes, for the mind. 
(Also v v important thing I’ve learnt - Life’s short to say no to a lovely cake!)

The mind's fuel is sleep. 

When the duo doesn't get what it's asking for — or when someone tries to hack it — it degrades. Sometimes suddenly, others gradually. Your anxiety is often just your mind working overtime, trying to protect you. Brain fog is often just an overworked body or mind.

It will happen. It's natural. The skill is not to never fall down; it's getting back up. And the fastest way back up is to trust the emotions, read the signal, and refuel well!

---

## What the system is for

Everything above is *how*. This is the *why.* And right now, I struggle to put it in words though.

The meaning of life is what you give it. You get to define how significant an experience is to you. 

It is afterall, your story to tell! 

— RM.
