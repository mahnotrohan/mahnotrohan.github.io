title: A Healthy Dose of Pessimism
date: 2026-10-18
---

I was talking to a friend last week about the CIRCLES framework. He was preparing for a PM interview, and while discussing it with him, I recalled the wonder and joy I felt when, a lustrum ago, I first learnt about the framework. It opened up a whole new realm for me. Now I could give shape to all my random ideas.

I have been a curious person since childhood, always wanting to know what’s under the hood, and being creative about new things — how to create them, how to experiment with them. The framework brought out a lot of optimism in me, because now I could articulate anything. Still, though, I had low confidence in how to actually implement those ideas.

That same week, at work, I was discussing with a colleague how I had developed a lot of pessimism about the business unit. It came with time. I kept showing up with optimism, and it kept getting hit by the reality of execution. What this led to was extreme pessimism towards things: when I couldn’t have a guarantee of things working out, I would push back on picking them up.

In hindsight, it’s not a good thing. If you push back a lot, you’re saying no to everything — even the ideas that would have worked. I have been thinking about it a lot. It’s so crucial to balance the pessimism. A healthy dose of pessimism is important and helps in making quick decisions, especially when a lot is on the line.

A friend and I were talking about life stuff, and we were discussing how, when enough life happens to you, you either become kind or become bitchy. The same goes for work. It’s a kinda important skill to have — actually, an art — to know when and how to balance pessimism with delusional optimism.
