title: Thoughts on Agentic Commerce
date: 2026-10-11
---

I think agentic commerce will grow. Marketplaces and stores will resist it at first. That is expected. But agents will become a new source of distribution and demand.

A general-purpose agent is a better way to shop. You state the goal once. The agent compares many options and completes the purchase.

This is a problem for marketplaces. Today they own the full funnel -

Intent → Search → Ads → Product → Purchase

With agents, the funnel becomes -

Intent → Agent → Connector → Purchase

Search matters less, and Ad revenue falls: the agent picks in one step, so there are fewer clicks and fewer ad slots.

Agents also bring new value. They send users who are close to buying, including users who would never have visited the store on their own. The trade-off is lost search and ad revenue against new agent traffic.

Once agent traffic is large enough, stores will take that trade. They can charge for access and use agent APIs and connectors as a new distribution channel.

D2C brands have more reason to open connectors than marketplaces do, so they will move first. Their flow becomes:

Brand → Agent → Customer

instead of:

Brand → Marketplace → Customer

The brand saves the commission, stops competing for rank, keeps the customer data, and owns the relationship. The agent does more than add a channel. It cuts the marketplace out. Vertical brands have one more edge: richer product data and context. For some purchases a specialist store beats a general marketplace.

That creates a flywheel:

More D2C connectors → More selection → Better agent UX → More users → More D2C traffic → More brands open connectors

Marketplaces will then have to join, or be the only closed channel while traffic moves elsewhere.

The likely end state splits control:

User → General-purpose agent → Connector → Merchant

The merchant keeps inventory, price, checkout, returns, and transaction rules. The agent owns intent, discovery, comparison, and decision.

I am bullish on Instinct's UX. Its value is less the model and more its role as a neutral layer between users and many stores. The test is whether it becomes a major owner of consumer intent. If it does, marketplaces will pay for access rather than lose the traffic.

Marketplaces can push back: build their own agents, block external ones, and protect the ad business for as long as they can. Google has an even stronger reason to protect search, and may make Gemini the agent. And marketplaces provide trust, fulfillment, returns, payments, and broad selection that most D2C stores lack. Agents have to replace enough of that.

The big shift is not from human search to AI search. It is from marketplace-owned distribution to agent-owned distribution.

Who owns consumer intent when shopping moves to agents?
