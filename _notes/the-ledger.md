A conflict you suppress stays where you put it. Over time it turns into resentment: the sense that you were treated unfairly, still sitting there.

Resentment stops telling disagreement apart from dismissal. Someone disagrees with your plan and you hear that you don't matter to them.

So you push harder. You say it louder. You bring up the older thing, because this time you have to be heard. What you earn is a reputation for being hard to talk to, and people give you less of a hearing than before. Arguments that used to be about the work now start with the dismissal already in them.

After that it runs on its own. Each move is a fair response to the one before it, which is what makes it so hard to point at and stop.

Stepping in the first time is cheap. It costs you one awkward conversation. By the third round the same conversation gets ugly and does damage.

That's rarely where you are when you finally see it. The cheap conversation is long behind you. What you hold instead is a ledger: a list of things that were unfair and never got acknowledged.

Now every discussion carries the urge to collect. A disagreement turns into a debate, the debate turns into something about the two of you, and the sharpness you bring is you going after the debt. Every attempt that fails adds a line to the ledger.

The admission you're waiting for isn't coming. Both of you are working from your own experience of what happened, and each account feels complete from the inside. The other person defends before they listen. That's their reflex. The apology you want would have to come from someone built differently. At some point you stop collecting, or the relationship ends.

You're carrying a map of how this is supposed to go, and somewhere on it is the day they admit it. Grief is redrawing that part of the map. You tell yourself the day isn't coming, and you keep telling yourself until you stop reaching for it.

Resentment is the refusal to redraw. You stand where your map and the world disagree and you insist the world has it wrong. It feels stronger than sadness, and that feeling of doing something is what keeps you standing there.

The write-off is done when you stop lending to someone who has never paid you back. You hear them defend themselves and it tells you something about them, and nothing about you.
