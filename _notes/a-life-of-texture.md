I barely travelled in the first two decades of my life. Trips were hard to come by growing up, and over time I talked myself out of wanting them. Why travel at all? Food was the only answer I could find.

So I cooked my way through everything I was curious about. What I couldn't cook, I asked friends to bring back from their trips. S brought me gifts from all over the world. Each one meant that, standing somewhere far away from home, he had remembered me.

The curiosity that should have pushed me out the door kept me in the kitchen.

The less I travelled and the more my friends did, the more I believed my own story, there was nothing out there to miss.

Then T came along, being herself, at full force. She inspired me. She made me want to go out and see for myself.

In December 2025, by chance, I started travelling every week. This March and April, I went to a village and to another country on barely a day of planning.

No number of pool swims could share an experience with what swimming in the sea is. Swimming with fishes, and getting swayed by waves, is a humbling experience after all

There's a scene in Good Will Hunting, by the pond. Robin Williams tells Will he could ask him about art, and Will would recite every book on it. But no book can tell him what it feels like to stand in the Sistine Chapel and look up at that ceiling. He's read about it. He hasn't smelled it.

It took me soooooooo long to really get it. I had been living my life through other people. You can read your way into a hundred lives and leave your own beliefs untouched. Someone who has lived through strange, first-hand things is different. What they've seen keeps testing what they believe, and forces them to rebuild.

At some point you have to go stand under the ceiling yourself.

That, I have grown to believe, is why travelling matters. It gives you a life of texture. The kind you only earn by showing up in person, letting a place happen to you, and coming home a little better at taking whatever life throws.
