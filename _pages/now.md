<div class="secth">Threads I keep pulling on</div>

<div class="now-list">
  <div class="now-item">
    <p class="q">How much of what happens to us is within our control?</p>
    <p>And how much do we just convince ourselves it is? I think about this in work, in relationships, in how I plan my days. I tend to be controlling, and I'm trying to understand where that instinct comes from and when to let go of it.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="now-item">
    <p class="q">What parts of who I am are actually mine?</p>
    <p>And what parts are inherited, borrowed, or performed? I notice this especially in how I present myself versus how I actually feel. I'm interested in the gap between the two.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="now-item">
    <p class="q">When is meaning-seeking generative, and when is it a trap?</p>
    <p>I'm drawn to people and work that feel meaningful — but suspicious of that instinct too. Not everything needs to mean something. Sometimes a run is just a run.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="now-item">
    <p class="q">How do tools shape thought?</p>
    <p>How framing changes decisions, and how the same person can reason clearly about one thing and be completely blind about another. I wrote about some of this in <a href="{{ site.baseurl }}/notes/pkms/">my PKMS essay</a>.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="now-item">
    <p class="q">Why is building so much more appealing than maintaining?</p>
    <p>I like making things — systems, frameworks, meals, this website. I'm curious about what makes building feel different from maintaining, and why I resist the latter.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="now-item">
    <p class="q">What makes a form factor click?</p>
    <p>I pay attention to how everyday objects are shaped — watches, rings, backpacks, bottles. Not in a design-nerd way; more why this thing feels good to use and that one doesn't.</p>
  </div>
</div>
