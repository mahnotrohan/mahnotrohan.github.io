      <h2 id="how-i-work-title">How I work</h2>
      <span class="small-meta">Updated 19 Sep 2026</span>
    </div>
 
  <p>Be direct with me, about my work or about yours. I&rsquo;d take a blunt correction over a compliment sandwich any day, and I try to return the favour. The two things that shut me down fastest are passive aggression and being managed rather than told.</p>
 
  <p>Ambiguity is my main source of friction. If a problem is fuzzy, the fastest way to unstick me is a concrete example of what good looks like and a couple of hard constraints. Given those, I&rsquo;m happy to figure out the rest, and I do my best work when I get to choose the approach and the success criteria.</p>
 
  <p>I close loops quickly, sometimes too quickly. Lots of open threads exhaust me, so I&rsquo;ll chase a decision down even if it means a partial answer now over a complete one later. Experiments, short cycles and fast feedback suit me; long maintenance work, slow feedback, and owning something without the authority to change it do not.</p>
 
  <p>My instinct is to reach for the data first: write a one-pager, state the hypotheses, then pull the numbers. I don&rsquo;t yet trust my gut the way I&rsquo;d like to, so building taste is something I&rsquo;m working on deliberately.</p>
 
  <p>I learn new things constantly, which is a strength that regularly costs me depth. I try to notice when I&rsquo;m skimming.</p>
 
  <p>I love turning messy problems into clear systems, and I&rsquo;d rather make something great than something perfect.</p>
 
  <p>Conflict works best for me when we sit down together, zoom out, and look at the problem rather than at each other. I&rsquo;m not naturally good at this and I&rsquo;m still learning.</p>
 
  <h3>Heuristics I default to</h3>
 
  <p><strong>Time-box it.</strong> Nothing gets finished without a deadline, including this page.</p>
  <p><strong>Aim for 80%.</strong> Perfect is a way of never shipping.</p>
  <p><strong>Explore, then exploit.</strong> When something works, double down hard. This does not apply to people; there, the urge to double down on what&rsquo;s familiar is exactly the bias to correct.</p>
  <p><strong>Do it right the first time, or measure and iterate fast.</strong> No third option.</p>
  <p><strong>Systems over tools.</strong> Low switching cost, intuitive flow, timelines that read left to right.</p>
  <p><strong>Close the loop.</strong> A partial learning I can act on beats a complete one still pending.</p>
  <p><strong>Process over outcome, consistency over intensity.</strong> Energy and mood fluctuate; the habits shouldn&rsquo;t.</p>

  <h3>My (many) flaws!</h3>
 
  <p>I fall for brain candy easily. I take things personally. I can be controlling. I&rsquo;m a perfectionist. And I overgeneralise. </p>
