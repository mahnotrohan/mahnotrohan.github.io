The Two Belief Trades

On the market tree, value and momentum share one branch: both are bets against the person on the other side. They just hold opposite priors about the same chart — prices return to worth versus prices keep going. The full creed of each, tested against twenty years of Indian tape.

§0

One chart, two creeds

the disagreement, drawn

Put a stock's market price on a chart next to a quiet dashed line — the slowly compounding worth of the underlying business. The two creeds transact on different features of this picture. Value acts on the vertical gap: how far price sits below worth. Momentum acts on the tangent: which way price is travelling, and how decisively. Everything else in this guide is the working-out of that one difference.

PART I

The tenets of value investing

Graham → Buffett → Dalal Street

Value investing is the older creed — codified by Benjamin Graham in the 1930s, industrialised by Warren Buffett, and practised in India by a long line from Chandrakant Sampat to Radhakishan Damani and the PMS value shops. Its tenets form a single logical chain: each one exists because the previous one is true.

V1

A share is a piece of a business, not a ticker

The founding move is a change of identity: you are not buying a symbol that wiggles, you are buying a fractional claim on inventories, brands, factories and future cash flows. Every later tenet depends on this — a wiggle has no "worth", but a business does.

Case · ITC, 2019–21

For three years the ticker went nowhere — the market was pricing FII outflows, ESG screens against cigarettes, and meme-grade despair ("ITC hai toh mumkin hai"). The business, meanwhile, kept compounding: Aashirvaad, Sunfeast and a cigarette franchise gushing cash at a ~15–16× multiple with a ~5–6% dividend yield. Owner and ticker had parted company — which is the entire opportunity.

V2

Mr. Market is your servant, not your guide

Graham's allegory: the market is a manic business partner who shouts a new price at you every day. Some days he's euphoric, some days suicidal. You are free to ignore him — his quote is an offer, never information about worth. "In the short run a voting machine, in the long run a weighing machine."

Case · March 2020

In one month the Nifty fell ~38%. Did the ten-year cash flows of Indian banking, cement and FMCG fall 38% in those thirty days? The value investor's claim is precisely that they did not — and whoever treated the quote as an offer rather than a verdict was buying HDFC Bank at ~₹770.

V3

Intrinsic value exists, and can be estimated — roughly

Discounted cash flows, normalised earnings power, replacement cost of assets — the method matters less than the humility: you are estimating a range, not a decimal. Better roughly right than precisely wrong. This is also where the creed forked: Graham valued assets (deep value, "cigar butts"); Buffett and Munger valued compounding quality — a wonderful business at a fair price over a fair business at a wonderful price. Both forks exist on Dalal Street today.

Case · Tata Motors, 2020

At the COVID trough the stock touched ~₹65 — a market cap that implied JLR was worth roughly nothing and the India CV cycle would never turn. Any sober sum-of-parts said otherwise. By 2024 the stock had crossed ₹1,000. The estimate never needed to be precise; it needed the market cap to be absurd against the range.

V4

Margin of safety — buy at a discount wide enough to survive being wrong

The most famous tenet, and the most misread. The gap between price and worth is not just your return — it is your insurance policy against your own errors, bad luck and a worse-than-expected future. Demand a discount so wide that a mediocre outcome still pays.

Case · Coal India, 2020–21

At ~4–5× earnings with a ~9–10% dividend yield, you were being paid to wait: even if the re-rating never came, the dividend alone beat fixed deposits. It came anyway — the stock roughly 4×'d by 2024. That's a margin of safety: heads you win, tails you win slowly.

V5

Bargains live where it hurts — contrarianism is structural, not stylistic

A stock gets mispriced downward only when owning it is embarrassing, painful or forbidden — hated sectors, forced sellers, institutional no-go zones. The discomfort is not a side effect of value investing; it is the source of the discount. If it feels comfortable, the gap has already closed.

Case · the PSU pack, 2020–24

In 2020, PSU stocks were institutionally "un-ownable" — governance discount, disinvestment overhang, decades of underperformance. SBI traded in the ₹150s below book value. By 2024 it crossed ₹800, and the same PSU basket that embarrassed portfolios became the market's darling. The discount existed because nobody could own it.

V6

Time arbitrage — patience is the actual edge

In a market of quarterly horizons, the durable advantage is simply a longer clock. The value investor buys from sellers who cannot wait — funds facing redemptions, traders facing stop-losses, institutions facing career risk — and charges them for immediacy in the currency of future returns. Note what this means: the edge is temperamental, not intellectual. The analysis is learnable; the waiting is not.

Case · ITC again, the resolution

The same stock that did nothing from 2019 to 2021 then roughly 2.4×'d in eighteen months as FMCG margins inflected and the hotels demerger surfaced value. The return was not earned in the eighteen months — it was earned in the three years of looking wrong.

V7

Cheap is not the same as mispriced — respect the value trap

The tenet that separates professionals from screen-runners. Some stocks are cheap on neglect (mispriced), others on decay (priced correctly for a shrinking future). The tests: is the balance sheet real? does profit convert to cash? is governance clean? is there a catalyst — buyback, demerger, cycle turn, new owner — or only hope? A falling P/E with falling earnings is a trapdoor, not a bargain.

Counter-cases · Yes Bank · Vodafone Idea

Yes Bank looked "cheap" the whole way from ₹300+ to under ₹20 — the book value it was cheap against was fiction. Vodafone Idea has spent years optically cheap while AGR liabilities consumed the equity. And remember the mirror image: Coal India spent 2015–20 looking like this exact trap before proving to be deep value. The line between trap and bargain is drawn by the balance sheet and the terminal value of the business — never by the multiple alone.

V8

Selling has rules too: exit when price meets worth, the thesis breaks, or a better gap appears

Value investing is symmetric: the same arithmetic that made you buy despair obliges you to sell euphoria. When price closes the gap to your estimate — or overshoots it — you are no longer holding a value position, just a position. This is why, in every cycle, the value seller and the momentum buyer meet at the same price and both feel clever.

Case · the 2023–24 PSU melt-up

The investors who bought PSUs at half of book in 2020 found them at 3–4× book in the 2024 euphoria — railway and defence names at multiples the businesses couldn't grow into. Value discipline meant selling into that strength, to buyers whose signal said "strongest stocks in the market." Both creeds executed correctly, on opposite sides of the same trade.

How value fails

Slowly, then all at once — and mostly by drought. Value's returns arrive in violent, unscheduled bursts after long deserts. In India the 2010–2020 decade belonged almost entirely to quality and growth; statistically cheap portfolios bled relative performance for years — long enough for clients to leave, funds to close, and the style to be declared dead, right before the 2021–24 value rally (the NSE's value index compounded at ~26% a year over the five years to mid-2026). The other failure is the trap (V7): cheapness that was truth, not opportunity. Value's risk is not volatility — it is being early for so long that you capitulate at the bottom.

PART II

The tenets of momentum investing

Jegadeesh–Titman → the NSE factor desk

Momentum is the younger, more unsettling creed — documented by Jegadeesh and Titman in 1993 (stocks that beat the market over the past 3–12 months keep doing so for the next few), and stubbornly present in a century of data across nearly every market studied. Nowhere has it worked better than in India, which is why the NSE now runs official momentum indices and every fund house sells one. Its tenets are fewer axioms, more operating procedures — because momentum is less a philosophy than a machine you must not interfere with.

M1

Trends persist — because humans underreact, then herd

The empirical core. News diffuses slowly: analysts anchor to old estimates, institutions build positions over quarters, and holders sell winners too early (the disposition effect) — all of which makes prices drift toward new information rather than jump. Then herding overshoots it. Momentum harvests the drift and aims to leave before the overshoot unwinds.

Evidence · India

The Nifty200 Momentum 30 index has compounded at ~18.6% a year (total return) since its April 2005 base — and an independent 18.5-year backtest that charges costs and taxes still finds ~14.0% vs the Nifty 50's ~10.4%. Among academic factors in India, momentum's premium has historically been the fattest — fatter than value's.

M2

Price is information — don't argue with the tape

The exact inversion of V2. Where Graham sees a manic partner to ignore, the momentum investor sees a voting machine that counts votes faster than any analyst — insiders, suppliers, channel checks, order books, all of it leaks into price first. A strong chart is someone else's research showing through.

Case · HAL & BEL, 2022

Defence stocks broke to new highs while most brokerage models still carried old order-book assumptions. The price was "telling" about indigenisation orders, export clearances and margin expansion months before consensus estimates caught up. HAL roughly 10×'d from its 2020 levels to the 2024 peak. The chart knew first — that is the tenet.

M3

Relative strength: own the leaders, whatever they are

Momentum is a ranking discipline, not a story discipline. Cross-sectional momentum ranks the whole universe by trailing return and owns the top slice — sector-agnostic, narrative-agnostic. (Its cousin, time-series or trend following, holds an asset only while it trades above its own trend.) The NSE recipe: score every Nifty 200 stock on 6-month and 12-month volatility-adjusted returns, own the top 30, reshuffle twice a year.

Case · the style's shapeshifting

The same momentum index has, at different rebalances, been stuffed with Adani infra (2022), PSU banks and railways (2023–24), defence (2024), and financials/pharma after the 2025 reset. No loyalty, no thesis, no sector view — only rank. A value investor holds a business; a momentum investor holds a position in a queue.

M4

The window matters: look back 3–12 months, skip the freshest move

Momentum lives at a specific frequency. Under ~1 month, prices mean-revert (last week's spike fades); beyond ~3 years they mean-revert again (long-run overreaction — which is where value lives). The classic construction is "12-minus-1": rank on the past year excluding the most recent month. Too short is noise; too long is a trend already dying.

Why it matters · a Monday spike is not momentum

Buying Monday's 15% gainer is short-term reversal territory — statistically you're the exit liquidity. Buying the stock that has quietly outperformed for nine months is momentum. Same instinct, opposite mathematics; the window is what separates the tenet from the tip.

M5

Ride winners, cut losers — invert your instincts

Humans sell winners to "lock in" gains and hold losers to avoid admitting error. Momentum's edge comes from doing the exact opposite: let the winner run far past where it feels expensive, and eject the loser mechanically the moment it drops from the ranking — no averaging down, ever. Averaging down is a value action justified by worth; in a momentum book it is simply disobedience.

Case · Trent, 2020–25

From ~₹600 to over ₹8,000 in four years — but only for holders who sat through it looking "expensive" the entire way (often 100×+ earnings). The same rulebook then forced the exit when relative strength collapsed in the 2024–25 correction, sparing the systematic holder the 40%+ drawdown that punished the loyal. Loyalty is a value virtue; in momentum it is a leak.

M6

Buy high, sell higher — the 52-week high is a signal, not a ceiling

Anchoring makes investors treat all-time highs as "too late", so they underreact to good news near highs — which is precisely why breakouts to new highs keep working. Feeling late is part of the trade's design; if it felt early, it would be value.

Case · Zomato, mid-2023

Reclaiming its ₹76 IPO price after the 2022 collapse "felt" like the last chance to exit at par — the anchoring trap. It was instead the breakout: the stock roughly 3×'d over the following eighteen months as profitability and the Blinkit story compounded the trend.

M7

Be systematic or don't bother

Every tenet above is psychologically unnatural — buying expensive-feeling stocks, selling small losses weekly, holding no opinions. Discretion will sand the edge off. So momentum is executed as a machine: fixed ranking rule, fixed rebalance calendar, position caps, wide diversification (30–50 names, because any single winner can implode). You are not picking stocks; you are operating a filter.

Case · the rebalance as an event

The NSE momentum indices reshuffle every June and December — mechanically, whatever the news. The December 2025 reconstitution alone was estimated to swing ~₹16,000 crore of passive money across entering and exiting stocks. The machine doesn't read the papers; the papers now read the machine.

M8

Survive the crash — momentum's edge pays for its tail

Momentum's dark tenet: the strategy earns steadily and loses catastrophically, precisely at regime turns. When a crashed market V-recovers, a momentum book is positioned exactly backwards — long yesterday's defensives, absent from the rebound. Costs compound the problem: high churn means impact costs, STT, and short-term capital-gains tax (20% vs 12.5% long-term, FY 2025–26), so the raw edge must out-earn its friction. Position caps, volatility scaling and the humility to expect a −50% someday are not add-ons; they are the strategy.

Cases · the graveyard

2008–09: the backtested momentum index drew down −70.5% and took 65 months to reclaim its high — the 2007 leaders (DLF, Unitech, Suzlon, Reliance Power) never came back at all. Jan 2023: Adani Enterprises sat near the top of momentum rankings when the Hindenburg report cut it ~60% in weeks. Sep 2024–Mar 2025: the smallcap/momentum complex fell hardest in the correction, giving back years of relative gains in months. Momentum without a survival plan is just leverage with better marketing.

How momentum fails

Suddenly, at the turn. The strategy is short one specific thing: regime change. It also decays with crowding — India's momentum AUM has exploded since 2021, and the more money chasing the same semi-annual reshuffle, the more the entry and exit prices move against it (front-running the rebalance is now its own cottage industry). And because pre-2020 index numbers are backtests, not live money, the marketing decks understate what 2008 felt like to actually hold. Momentum's risk is not being wrong about a business — it owns no view of any business — it is being positioned for yesterday on the day the weather flips.

§ FIN

Must you choose?

yin, yang, and the rebalance

No — and the best evidence says you shouldn't. Because the two creeds hold opposite priors, their bad years rarely coincide: value's droughts were momentum's feasts (2017, 2019–20), and momentum's crashes were value's harvests (2009, arguably 2025). Cliff Asness calls them the yin and yang of investing; AQR's famous result is that a simple 50/50 of the two factors beats either alone, everywhere it's been tested — the negative correlation means the blend earns both premia with less pain, and rebalancing between them forces you to sell whichever creed just had its party.

The honest summary

Value is a bet that other people overreact; momentum is a bet that they underreact — and both are true, at different horizons. Overreaction dominates at long horizons (3–5 years: despair overshoots, hence value), underreaction at medium ones (3–12 months: news drifts in slowly, hence momentum). That is why both premia can exist in the same market at the same time without contradiction.

But never forget which branch of the tree you are standing on. Both are belief trades — zero-sum against the person opposite. Value's counterparty is the panicked or constrained seller; momentum's is the anchored doubter and the late herd. Before every trade, the first-principles question stands: who is on the other side, and why am I the one who's right? If you have no answer, the time branch of the tree — the index fund — is not a consolation prize; it is the only trade that never needs one.

This guide is education, not investment advice or a recommendation — factor returns are historical, backtest-heavy, and taxes/regulations change. Companion to Anatomy of a Market, the first field guide in this series.
